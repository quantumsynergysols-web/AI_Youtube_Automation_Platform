Design note/19 August 2026/Standalone product

Architecture & Delivery Method

The system design for the AI YouTube automation platform, expressed as UML, and a reasoned choice of software engineering method for the 17-week build. Companion to the SRS v1.1.

Part 1

Architecture

1.1 Deployment view

The product is standalone, so nothing is shared with Quantum Framer at runtime. It has its own VPS, its own database, its own storage bucket and its own Stripe account. The single most important structural decision is visible here: topic research goes to a third-party data vendor, not to the YouTube API. Sending research through YouTube would cap the whole platform at roughly 15 videos a day regardless of how many customers sign up.

1.2 Component view

Every external provider sits behind an adapter. That is not architectural decoration — provider pricing moved materially in the last year, and the SRS commits to substitutability as a non-functional requirement. The Originality Guard is drawn on the critical path on purpose: it is a gate, not a report.

Part 3

Engineering method

Recommendation

Iterative-incremental delivery with phase gates, two-week iterations and Kanban flow inside each phase.

Not Waterfall, because the environment moves faster than the plan. Not Scrum by the book, because the ceremony assumes a team two to three times this size. The method takes the phase structure the SRS already defines, adds a fixed feedback cadence, and puts a go/no-go decision in the team lead's hands at each boundary.

3.1 What the choice actually turns on

Method selection here is not a matter of taste. Four properties of this specific project decide it:

  • Requirements are stable; the environment is not. The SRS specifies 55 requirements and they are unlikely to churn. But YouTube's monetisation policy changed in July 2026, upload quota costs changed in December 2025, and video model pricing moved repeatedly through the year. A method that freezes a plan against a moving external world will be wrong by month three.
  • The two riskiest questions are empirical. Is the generated output good enough that viewers actually watch it? Will established creators pay $79 a month? Neither can be answered by analysis. Both need something real in front of someone, early.
  • The team is small. Assumed two to four people, with the team lead doubling as product owner and reviewer. Process weight has to be proportionate.
  • The date is fixed but the scope is negotiable. 17 weeks with P0/P1/P2 priorities already assigned — which is exactly the shape that incremental delivery handles and fixed-scope methods do not.

3.2 Methods considered

MethodFitAssessment
Waterfall Rejected Produces no working software until roughly week 14. The quality and pricing questions would stay unanswered until it is too late to change course, and external API behaviour cannot be discovered from a document.
V-Model Rejected as a whole Inherits Waterfall's late feedback and adds test documentation weight a small team cannot carry. Its verification discipline is borrowed for FR-9 only — the Originality Guard protects the customer's income, so acceptance tests there are written before the implementation.
Spiral Partly adopted The risk-first instinct is exactly right, but formal risk analysis per cycle is disproportionate to a 17-week build. The risk spike is adopted; the framework is not.
Scrum, by the book Rejected Three roles and five events presuppose five to nine people and a Product Owner distinct from the team. Here one person is lead, owner and reviewer, so the ceremony generates overhead without generating signal.
Extreme Programming Partly adopted Pair programming and an on-site customer are impractical at this size. Continuous integration, small releases, refactoring and test-first on the compliance layer are adopted directly.
Kanban Partly adopted Strong on flow and WIP limits for a small team, weak at committing to a fixed date. Used as the mechanism inside a phase, not as the whole method.
Iterative-incremental with phase gates Selected Phases 0–6 are already natural increments, each demonstrable. Two-week iterations force a feedback rhythm; gates give explicit go/no-go points. Fits the team size, the fixed date and the negotiable scope, and gets the risky questions answered in the first month.

3.3 How it maps onto the 17 weeks

The important structure is not the phase bars — those come from the SRS. It is the two parallel tracks beneath them, and the fact that both report back before the expensive phase starts.

3.4 Gate criteria

A gate is a decision, not a milestone. Each has an exit condition that can be demonstrated, and the team lead can hold, re-scope or proceed.

GateWeekExit condition
G03A user signs up, subscribes through Stripe in test mode, and a dummy job runs end to end through the queue.
G15A real YouTube channel connects by OAuth, tokens survive a refresh cycle, and history imports.
G29A complete short-form video is generated from a topic with no manual steps beyond the two human checkpoints, at a measured cost within 20% of $0.96.
G311Metadata, chapters, captions and three thumbnail concepts generate for that video.
G413The guard blocks a deliberately duplicated script and passes a genuine one; acceptance tests written before implementation all pass.
G515A video uploads and schedules to a real channel, and a forced quota error recovers by backoff without loss.
G617Metrics ingest nightly, the dashboard shows performance against baseline, and recommendations generate. Public release.

3.6 Definition of done

A requirement is done when all of the following hold — not when the code merges:

  • Behaviour matches the FR as written, or the FR has been formally amended in the change log.
  • Automated tests cover the success path and the known failure paths, and run in CI.
  • Provider calls go through an adapter, never directly, so the provider stays substitutable.
  • Cost per invocation is recorded to UsageRecord where the operation spends money.
  • Failure states surface to the user with a remedy, and to admin with a cause.
  • It has been demonstrated on staging with real provider credentials, not mocks alone.

3.7 The one thing this method does not solve

Process cannot fix capacity. Risk R-8 in the SRS records that the team is simultaneously committed to Quantum Framer, three weeks into its own build-and-publish window. Two-week iterations will make that collision visible early and honestly, which is worth something — but they will not resolve it.

The gate structure gives the team lead a legitimate place to stop. If G0 or G2 slips because attention is split, that is the signal to sequence the two products rather than run them concurrently.
